People Operations built from zero

Ridecell | Dec 2018 - Jan 2026

Challenge
Ridecell had no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount lived in spreadsheets, and employees had no tracked channel for questions.
What I did
I built a library of 20+ standard operating procedures with named owners and annual reviews, launched ASKPeople on Jira and Confluence with tiered SLAs, and made Sapling the HR single source of truth, integrated with ADP and Zapier.
Result
The company grew from 3 countries to 5, supported 6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits, cut approval cycles from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and resolved 5-10 tickets a day in 24-48 hours.
What I would do differently
I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge
HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process, which created audit risk. Early on, some people said a form would be too process-heavy.
What I did
I built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form that requires documentation and approval before any HRIS change, plus Notion New Hire and Offboarding workflows with an owner on every task.
Result
100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed, and preboarding triggered within 24 hours of offer acceptance.
What I would do differently
I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

  • Notion

  • n8n
  • Rippling

A reorganization with cost visibility

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge
A reorganization affecting 18 employees across the US and India needed accurate severance, COBRA costs, and agreements, fast.
What I did
I built an AI-enabled severance calculator and drafter and a RIF cost model with Claude and Gemini. It calculated severance, COBRA cost, and COBRA months owed, drafted agreements, and tracked every step on a shared spreadsheet for Payroll and IT.
Result
Leadership got cost visibility it had never had, and agreement prep dropped from about 20 minutes per employee to about 3 hours total.
What I would do differently
I'd have built the cost model before the reorganization was decided. Leaders could then have compared scenarios rather than just seeing the cost of the chosen one.

  • Google Sheets

How I would start

A proposed plan built around the success picture in the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet People Team leaders and process owners to learn how work gets done today.
  • Start the process inventory: services, systems, owners, handoffs, pain points, and risks.
  • Learn the HRIS replacement and People Team shared front door plans.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Prioritize opportunities by business impact, team-member experience, risk, cycle time, and effort.
  • Map one or two high-friction workflows end to end and find the root causes.
  • Set up a lightweight PMO rhythm: roadmap, status reporting, and action tracking.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Run a first pilot with its process owner, including communications and training.
  • Set baselines for cycle time, quality, and adoption before launch.
  • Document the new process as an SOP with a decision record, and gather feedback.
By month 6

The outcome

  • A credible, usable inventory of priority People processes with clear ownership.
  • A transparent roadmap leaders can use to see progress, tradeoffs, and risks.
  • Priority workflows that are simpler, better documented, measurable, and adopted.

This is a starting point. I would adjust it after the first conversations with the hiring manager and People Team leaders.
